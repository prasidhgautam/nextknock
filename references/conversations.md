# Turn replies into useful conversations

Use this reference when the user requests help with an actual reply or an upcoming conversation. Read the relevant thread and the current brief first. Follow [outreach](outreach.md) for account checks, source verification, drafting, and preserving thread context. Replies and recaps remain unsent drafts by default. A positive response is not an interview or offer unless it actually says so.

## Respond to what was asked

- **“Send your portfolio/CV.”** Select the approved current version and relevant authorized samples. Briefly explain why the chosen sample matters. If there is no portfolio, say so plainly and offer a permitted alternative such as a concise project explanation. Do not fabricate a public URL or expose confidential work.
- **“Tell me more about your experience.”** Select one or two relevant evidence-bank examples. Prepare a concise account of context, the candidate's own actions, supported results, and what they learned. Do not convert team outcomes into personal metrics.
- **“When can we talk?”** Use confirmed availability and timezone. If missing, prepare the rest of the reply and ask the user for suitable times. Do not send, book, or promise availability merely because the recipient suggested a call.
- **A referral or introduction.** Thank the person and prepare context that is easy to forward. A proposed introduction does not authorize contacting the new person, sharing their details, or forwarding the full thread. Follow the user's requested scope.
- **Rejection or opt-out.** Record the outcome and stop follow-ups. Help draft a brief acknowledgment only if requested; do not turn it into a renewed pitch.

## Prepare for a call

Build a short private brief from the agreed purpose and real thread: who is involved and what is known about their role, what the user hopes to learn, relevant company context, two or three evidence-backed stories if useful, and questions connected to the work. Label guesses and research gaps. Distinguish an exploratory conversation from a formal interview.

Tailor questions to the person. Useful subjects include what success in the role involves, difficult tradeoffs, team responsibilities, and the evidence they would want from a candidate making this transition. Avoid asking questions already answered in the thread or public job description. Offer practice when requested without inventing what the actual recipient will ask.

## After a conversation

Use the user's notes or an authorized transcript. Ask for missing notes rather than claiming the call occurred or inventing commitments. Distinguish statements, tentative possibilities, and agreed actions with owners and dates. Draft a concise recap when requested, mentioning something actually discussed and the agreed next step. Obtain the user's answer before promising new work or a deadline.

Record a conversation as completed only from the user's confirmation or appropriate evidence. Record introductions, interview invitations, offers, and deadlines separately. A scheduled call is not a completed call; a polite reply is not hiring interest.
