# Outreach that earns a conversation

## Research before writing

Identify an appropriate recipient: the actual recruiter, relevant functional leader, or a founder who plausibly owns hiring. Verify identity and professional contact details using the vacancy, official sources, permitted professional information, or an existing thread. Do not guess email patterns. Choose contact coverage appropriate to the user's scoped workflow. For [LinkedIn opportunity networking](linkedin.md), research 2–3 relevant functional people and 1–2 recruiting/HR people where justified, with a distinct reason for each person to care. For other outreach, a focused initial contact is often sufficient. Never send several colleagues the same pitch or repeated referral requests. A suitable conversation can be exploratory when no vacancy exists, but never imply there is an open role without evidence.

Understand what the company does, whom it serves, the recipient's relevance, and where the candidate's evidence connects. Seek a meaningful company development in the preceding 12 months relative to the current date: a product release, expansion, initiative, campaign, technical milestone, or substantive interview. Prefer original company announcements and first-hand material; use credible reporting when needed. Verify the event date as well as publication date so a repost does not make old news recent.

Save source URLs, dates, the factual observation, and why it matters to this candidate. If no relevant recent development is verifiable, use an observed product, business, or role detail. Tell the user about the research limitation; do not invent news or put an awkward research disclaimer in the recipient's message. If research access is insufficient, prepare a clearly labelled local draft for review and identify the missing verification before creating an addressed Gmail draft.

Before drafting, make a short private connection note: **observation → relevance to this recipient's work → supported candidate proof → a useful question or tentative contribution**. Distinguish the sourced fact from your interpretation. If the only connection is that the company made news, choose a more relevant product or role detail, or explain that the evidence is insufficient. Recent news is an option, not a quota. [Annotated fictional examples](outreach-examples.md) show how to make this connection without supplying a universal email template.

## Write with a reason to respond

Choose an angle that fits the recipient and the candidate's own voice. Do not prescribe a universal sequence or reuse a finished email across companies. Initial emails will usually work in 80–140 words; clarity and substance matter more than a word target. Channel limits and thread context may call for shorter text.

Use the preferences from [voice calibration](evidence-and-voice.md). If the user has not supplied any and wants an immediate draft, write plainly and let them correct the tone. Match the most relevant evidence to this message instead of listing every achievement.

Humanize through the candidate's natural phrasing, a concrete observation, honest contribution, and consideration for this person's work. A functional leader may care about a work tradeoff; a recruiter may care about role relevance and credible scope. Do not swap names in an otherwise identical finished message. A connection note establishes relevance briefly; a post-acceptance pitch or eligible direct message can develop the connection. Neither an acceptance nor a Premium badge means a message was sent or a reply is owed.

Appreciate something specific and explain what is interesting about it. Avoid decorative praise or a news reference pasted onto an unrelated job pitch. Connect the observation to one relevant proof from the candidate's CV or portfolio. A useful idea may create interest, but label it as a possibility rather than diagnosing the company or promising a result.

After each two or three lines, edit from the recipient's perspective:

- Why does this matter to me, my work, or my company?
- What has this person noticed or done that makes a reply worthwhile?
- Does the next sentence add evidence, a useful thought, or clarity?

These are editing questions, not rhetorical questions to insert in the email. Remove lines that only announce enthusiasm, repeat the CV, or demand attention. Creative attention comes from a sharp observation and relevant proof, not mystery subjects, false urgency, manufactured familiarity, or pressure.

Use an honest, specific subject. Close with one easy invitation: a relevant question or a short conversation with a clear purpose. Do not ask a stranger to solve the entire job search or assume they will refer the candidate. No message can force a response.

Avoid stock openings such as “I hope this email finds you well,” “esteemed organization,” “perfect fit,” and “unlock your potential.” Do not claim to be a customer, admirer, or long-time follower unless the user has established that. Never turn confidential portfolio information into a public link without authorization.

Replies should answer the actual question first. Do not insert a cold introduction into an existing recruiter conversation. Ask the user for missing compensation, availability, or interview commitments rather than guessing.

For substantive replies, introductions, call preparation, and recaps, read [conversation support](conversations.md). Keep reply preparation under the same draft and account boundaries as initial outreach.

## Save Gmail drafts

Creating drafts requires a request to prepare outreach in that account; discussing a career move alone is insufficient. Verify the selected Gmail identity using the available connector/profile or observed browser account. Check relevant sent messages, pending drafts, replies, and cross-channel contact before creating a new draft. If some history is inaccessible, disclose the coverage gap and ask about prior contact where it could cause duplication.

Use a supported Gmail draft connector when available, otherwise documented browser controls. Keep every draft unsent. Use actual reply/thread identifiers for replies. Attach only the user's approved document version when appropriate, and include only authorized portfolio links. Avoid unnecessary attachments in a brief first contact.

Record `drafting` before creation. Verify the saved draft's recipient, subject, body, reply context, and attachments via the returned details or a supported read/list/UI inspection. Return the real draft ID/reference and an accessible link only when supplied or observed; never invent a Gmail deep link. A draft ID alone is useful when no direct link exists. Record `draft_saved` only for confirmed persistence. If uncertain, use `needs_verification` and inspect existing drafts before retrying.

Reuse the same recorded unsent draft for revisions when supported. Preserve user edits and attachments; if replacing MIME content, include intended attachments and relevant history. Do not overwrite a draft the user has changed without reconciling those edits. If an attachment cannot be added, say so, record the manual step, and do not describe the draft as fully ready.

If Gmail is unavailable, provide the complete recipient (when verified), subject, body, and attachment instructions locally, with accurate connection/manual steps. Record `draft_prepared`, not `draft_saved`. Do not substitute another account or silently send through another service. The default completion is a draft for the user to review and send manually.

## Follow-ups and conversation outcomes

Check the latest relevant thread and actual sent state first, including messages sent manually by the user. An unsent draft does not start a follow-up clock. Respect a promised response date; otherwise use five business days after the last sent message for a first follow-up, then seven business days after the first sent follow-up for a second. These are editable review defaults, using weekdays unless a verified holiday calendar is available.

Reconcile LinkedIn invitations, InMail, direct messages, and email together. A sent invitation or acceptance alone does not start the pitch follow-up clock. An accepted connection must not trigger another initial pitch if one was already sent through another route. Apply rejection, opt-out, and unanswered-follow-up stops across channels and all linked vacancies; never switch routes to evade a stop.

Add a relevant thought or answer the pending question; avoid empty “just checking in” reminders. Do not reintroduce the candidate as though there were no prior contact. Stop on rejection, opt-out, closure, or after two unanswered sent follow-ups. A received reply takes priority over a planned nudge. If the role is closed, do not turn a rejection into a fresh speculative pitch without a distinct user request and legitimate context.

Record human replies and conversations separately from automated acknowledgements. A saved draft is preparation, and a sent email alone does not prove employer receipt or an application. Track meaningful conversations without equating any reply with hiring interest.
