# Fictional walkthrough: one NextKnock, several focused roles

**Offline synthetic demonstration only.** Casey Lin and SolsticeGrid are invented. Reference date: 2026-10-18. All example URLs use `.example`; no account is connected and no invitation, message, application, or monitoring schedule is created by this walkthrough.

## Start with one invocation

> $nextknock — Here is my CV. I cannot share a portfolio because my employer work is confidential. I want more business collaboration in an operations data role. After we agree on direction, I would like to use LinkedIn for suitable opportunities, with thoughtful networking around each application. Please help me understand what you can do and what I must do manually.

The coordinator first reviews Casey's supported dashboard and category-analysis work, confirms the career direction and missing constraints, and asks for platform/account choices afterward. It does not turn the CV into invented commercial impact. Casey's permission to review the CV does not itself authorize external actions or monitoring.

## Divide responsibility while keeping one record

Under the same skill, focused agents can research the vacancy, find and qualify people, draft individually, and check sources and duplicates. The coordinator reconciles their evidence, owns the private canonical tracker, and controls dependent actions. Agents do not each contact the same person or maintain conflicting final ledgers. If parallel agents are unavailable, perform those roles sequentially.

See [coordination](../references/coordination.md), [LinkedIn applications and networking](../references/linkedin.md), and [acceptance monitoring](../references/monitoring.md) for the operating rules.

For SG-41, the role researcher finds a credible connection to Casey's dashboard maintenance and operational-category work. The contact researcher recommends Morgan and Leena from Operations Analytics plus Devon from talent. Avery is a possible colleague, but the evidence does not establish hiring influence. Priya is excluded because of an opt-out. The CEO is not selected just for seniority. Devon is one HR person, with department alignment recorded once. The coordinator reports the available coverage honestly instead of manufacturing extra people to meet a quota.

SG-42 involves the same team. Morgan and Devon remain the same people across both opportunities; a second job does not restart contact history. Leena's invitation is already pending and must not be repeated.

## Keep the application and the conversation independently truthful

SG-41's Easy Apply form is prepared. SG-42's external ATS timed out after a previous Submit. Neither a prepared form nor an uncertain timeout proves an application succeeded. Because this demonstration has no permitted live operation, the coordinator prepares the necessary material, records the exact manual steps, and asks for a receipt or observed application record before counting completion. It does not retry SG-42 blindly.

For this example, a private source note connects SolsticeGrid's shared exception-review workspace launch (event 2026-05-27, publication 2026-06-03) with Casey's actual experience checking category inconsistencies with operations colleagues. This is fictional source evidence; it does not establish a real vacancy, real product use, or business outcomes.

## Make each person's reason to care different

These are local drafts, not sent messages. They are writing examples, not templates to reuse verbatim. A platform's actual character limits still need checking.

**Department leader opening:**

> Morgan, your post about category definitions staying usable for operations reviewers caught my attention. That was a recurring part of my dashboard work: checking inconsistent labels with the people who used them. I’m exploring your operations analyst opening and would value a connection.

**HR opening:**

> Devon, I’m exploring SolsticeGrid’s operations analyst opening. My experience is in dashboard maintenance and checking ticket-category inconsistencies with operations colleagues. Your analytics hiring focus looks relevant; I’d be glad to connect and understand the team’s priorities.

The first gives a department leader a specific shared problem; the second gives a recruiter a concise, honest fit signal. Neither claims the recipient controls hiring, asks for a referral immediately, or invents results. Review every few lines: why would this person care, and what would make a response worthwhile?

**After an independently verified acceptance, with no prior message:**

> Thanks for connecting, Morgan. SolsticeGrid’s shared exception-review workspace caught my attention because it brings analysts and operations reviewers into the same discussion about category changes.
>
> In my current role, I maintain an exception dashboard and check inconsistent labels with operations colleagues. I owned the analysis; the team decided which process changes to make. That is the kind of collaboration I’d like more of in my next role.
>
> I’m exploring the Operations Data Analyst opening. From your perspective, is the bigger challenge spotting exceptions or agreeing what the categories mean? Even a brief pointer would help me understand the work better.

If the source packet instead shows an InMail was already sent to Morgan yesterday, acceptance only updates the existing history. It does not justify sending this opening again. Premium status alone does not prove usable InMail credits. An Open Profile route, when actually available, still requires a supported and authorized operation.

## Continue honestly when the turn ends

No scheduler or supported acceptance access is available in this demonstration. NextKnock says so, saves the profile-specific stopping point outside the installed skill, and asks the user to return with the exact accepted profile observation. It cannot promise to watch in the background. Before resuming, it checks identity, the actual state, prior messages, opt-outs, and the freshness of the source. The example record in [synthetic LinkedIn state](../evals/linkedin-state.md) is a setup illustration, not an automation.
